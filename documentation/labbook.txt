ACEpIC: Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme peptide Inhibitory Concentration Predictor

ACEpIC: Architectural Enhancements & Methodology Specification
Continuous pIC50 Bioactivity Regression for Peptide Design

1. OVERVIEW & METHODOLOGICAL ETHICAL STANDARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Adapting and enhancing established machine learning architectures—such as
transitioning pLM4ACE from a binary classification model into a continuous pIC50
regression pipeline—is a standard, scientifically rigorous, and fully ethical
practice in computational biology and computer science.

By properly attributing foundational works (e.g., the pLM4ACE framework) and
clearly highlighting novelty, novel loss functions, and structural integrations,
this project constitutes an original and high-value research contribution.

2. STATE-OF-THE-ART (SOTA) ARCHITECTURAL ENHANCEMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To convert a binary active/inactive classifier into a high-precision continuous
pIC50 regression framework [where pIC50 = -log10(IC50 in M)], five key modular
upgrades are integrated based on recent deep learning advances:

2.1 Backbone Modernization (Next-Gen Protein Language Models)
    * Beyond ProtBERT: Replacing legacy protein language models (pLMs) with state-of-
      the-art embeddings.
    * ESM-2 (esm2_t33_650M_UR50D): Provides significantly richer biological, structural,
      and evolutionary representations across amino acid sequences.
    * ProstT5: Generates dual sequence-structure embeddings by predicting Foldseek
      structural tokens alongside sequence tokens. This provides implicit 3D geometric
      awareness without requiring computationally expensive AlphaFold structures.

2.2 Hybrid Architecture: Sequence + Structure (pLM + GNN)
    * Addressing Representation Blind Spots: Sequence-only pLMs miss explicit 3D
      conformational and topological spatial details critical for IC50 precision.
    * Implementation Pipeline:
      1. Extract per-residue sequence embeddings using ESM-2.
      2. Construct a peptide molecular graph (Nodes = residues/atoms; Edges = bonds
         or spatial distance cutoffs).
      3. Pass the graph representation through a Graph Attention Network (GAT) or
         Graph Convolutional Network (GCN).
      4. Concatenate pLM sequence vectors with GNN structural vectors prior to
         the final regression head.

2.3 Dual-Channel Interaction Modeling (Proteochemometrics & Cross-Attention)
    * Context-Aware Bioactivity: IC50 values describe binding affinity toward a
      specific biological target (e.g., ACE enzyme). Sequence-only models predict
      target-averaged values.
    * Dual-Encoder Innovation:
      1. Encoder 1 processes the candidate peptide sequence.
      2. Encoder 2 processes the target protein sequence or binding pocket.
    * Mechanism: A Cross-Attention layer is deployed to dynamically learn residue-level
      pairwise interaction maps between peptide motifs and target binding sites.

2.4 Hybrid Loss Function: Precision & Ranking Optimization
    * Beyond Standard MSE: Standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) optimizes purely for
      absolute numerical error. In drug discovery, correct relative ranking (potency
      ordering) is often more valuable than exact numerical values.
    * Formulation: Combine MSE with a pairwise ranking loss (Concordance Index Loss
      or Pairwise Margin Loss):

      Loss_total = α * Loss_MSE + (1 - α) * Loss_ranking

      (where α ∈ [0, 1] balances numerical error and ranking consistency).

2.5 Uncertainty Quantification (UQ)
    * Overcoming Overconfidence: Deep neural networks tend to yield overconfident
      point estimates even on out-of-distribution (OOD) test data.
    * Innovation: Integrate Monte Carlo Dropout (MC Dropout) or Evidential Deep
      Regression into the head architecture.
    * Output: The network outputs both a predicted potency mean (μ = pIC50) and
      an estimated variance/standard deviation (± σ), delivering actionable confidence
      bounds for experimental validation.
